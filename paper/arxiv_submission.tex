\title{Short Addition Completions in GPT-2 Small: Behavioral Limits and a Target-Dependent Equal-Operand Preference}
\author{Anay Katiyar\\\small Independent Researcher}
\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Mechanistic studies have examined arithmetic in pretrained language models, but evidence from those models and tasks does not by itself establish how GPT-2 Small scores short decimal-addition completions. We present a bounded audit of saved behavioral, attribution, intervention, and prompt-comparison results for GPT-2 Small (124M parameters). In a held-out zero-shot cohort, top-1 accuracy is 2/32, compared with 8/32 for the best constant guess. In a 79-cell prompt grid, the sign of a neighbor-averaged target logit contrast agrees with target parity in 68/79 cells, with substantially different agreement across target ranges. A scan of seven even target sums records a mean target-level equal-operand advantage of +0.2474 (exact sign-flip $p=0.03125$; target-bootstrap 95\% interval $[+0.0738,+0.4374]$), positive at six targets. Correcting final-LayerNorm scaling and separating unembedding bias narrows earlier component interpretations; candidate-component screens remain exploratory (nominated-candidate one-sided FDR $q=0.09375$, two-sided $q=0.15$) and do not establish a circuit. A digit+digit versus digit+word target-level contrast survives Holm correction ($p=0.046875$), while plus-versus-other operator comparisons do not detect a difference after correction. Archived raw corpus-proxy logs contain 79 requests with no recorded errors; their no-floor target summary is descriptive and does not report an association test. The study's contribution is a model- and prompt-specific characterization of these measurements and their interpretive limits, including an exploratory equal-operand pattern; it does not identify an addition mechanism or establish that arithmetic representations are absent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Mechanistic interpretability seeks explanations of model behavior in terms of internal representations and computations, rather than correlations between inputs and outputs alone \cite{elhage2021framework}. GPT-2 is an autoregressive language model trained on next-token prediction \cite{radford2019gpt2}; arithmetic is an attractive test case because the intended operation is explicit, but a model's preference for a correct answer token is not by itself evidence that it carried out the operation.

The closest comparison by model is Hanna et al.'s study of greater-than prediction in pretrained GPT-2 Small \cite{hanna2023greater}. They identify and test a circuit for comparing year-like values, rather than decimal addition. By task, more direct precedents study arithmetic mechanisms in pretrained language models. Stolfo et al. use causal mediation to trace arithmetic-related information flow across models with 2.8B--7B parameters \cite{stolfo2023causal}; Yu and Ananiadou analyze addition and other operations in Llama-7B and GPT-J, including attention heads and feed-forward neurons \cite{yu2024cna}; and Nikankin et al. characterize arithmetic circuits and heuristic neurons in pretrained Llama 3, Pythia, and GPT-J models \cite{nikankin2025heuristics}. These studies establish that mechanistic arithmetic analysis in pretrained models is not new. Their models, scales, prompt designs, and analyses differ from the short GPT-2 Small decimal-completion probes reported here, so their circuit findings neither validate nor rule out the interpretations tested in this study. Nanda et al. and Zhong et al. provide a further comparison through transformers trained on modular addition, where learned algorithms can be examined in controlled settings \cite{nanda2023progress,zhong2023clock}. Ye et al. study models trained on synthetic grade-school math, another distinct setting \cite{ye2024physics}.

This work audits a set of GPT-2 Small probes on prompts such as \texttt{3 + 5 =}. The initial hypothesis concerned a general addition-specific circuit. Behavioral baselines, attribution corrections, and intervention controls narrowed that interpretation. A target-dependent preference for equal-operand prompts remained in a seven-target scan, so we report it as a descriptive behavioral effect and a candidate for future causal testing.

The contribution is deliberately narrower than a new account of arithmetic mechanisms: we document how exact-answer baselines, a neighbor-token logit contrast, and an equal-operand comparison behave in this specific GPT-2 Small prompt setting, and show how attribution and intervention audits constrain circuit-level interpretations. The equal-operand pattern is exploratory and descriptive. We do not claim priority for mechanistic arithmetic research, identify an addition algorithm, or infer that GPT-2 Small lacks arithmetic-related representations. This focused comparison is not a systematic review of all arithmetic-interpretability work.

\section{Study scope and related work}

\paragraph{Pretrained mathematical behavior in GPT-2 Small.} Hanna et al. examine a greater-than task in pretrained GPT-2 Small and validate a circuit across related contexts \cite{hanna2023greater}. This is the nearest precedent by model family, not by arithmetic operation. The present study examines decimal-addition prompts and does not provide comparable circuit validation or generalization evidence.

\paragraph{Mechanistic studies of pretrained arithmetic.} Prior work has directly investigated arithmetic computations in pretrained models. Stolfo et al. use causal mediation on arithmetic questions and report information transfer from operands and operators toward the answer position, followed by result-related processing in MLP modules \cite{stolfo2023causal}. Yu and Ananiadou study arithmetic heads and feed-forward neurons in Llama-7B and report related analyses in GPT-J \cite{yu2024cna}. Nikankin et al. analyze arithmetic circuits across pretrained Llama 3, Pythia, and GPT-J models and characterize collections of heuristic neurons that contribute to answer logits \cite{nikankin2025heuristics}. These are direct precedents for mechanistic arithmetic analysis and for the broader question of algorithmic computation versus narrower patterns. The present work is not a replication of those studies: it uses GPT-2 Small, short next-token decimal prompts, a low-accuracy behavioral setting, and an equal-operand contrast. Its narrower contribution is to document that contrast and the limits of the saved evidence available to interpret it. Differences in models and methods prevent treating either set of findings as confirmation of the other.

\paragraph{Arithmetic in trained models and other tasks.} Nanda et al. study small transformers trained on modular addition and use activation, weight, and intervention analyses to validate a learned Fourier algorithm \cite{nanda2023progress}. Zhong et al. show that learned modular-addition solutions vary with architecture and training conditions \cite{zhong2023clock}. Lee et al. study how training data and formats affect arithmetic learning in small transformers \cite{lee2024teaching}. Ye et al. analyze internal processes in synthetic grade-school math settings, including GPT-2-family models trained on generated data \cite{ye2024physics}. These studies offer useful comparisons, but their training regimes or tasks differ from the pretrained short-completion setting here.

\paragraph{Circuit-evaluation methods.} Work on indirect-object identification and automated circuit discovery motivates explicit behavioral metrics and causal validation \cite{wang2023ioi,conmy2023acdc}. These papers supply methodological context; all measurements below come from this project's saved artifacts.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Model, prompts, and saved records}

The reported model is GPT-2 Small (124M parameters). Prompts use short textual arithmetic forms, principally \texttt{a + b =}. The repository preserves the full-record notebook, the supplied clean-rerun output tables, raw corpus query logs, a 79-row addition grid, a 39-row digit-addition double/control score file, and a checksummed artifact manifest. The exported format and operator tables report target-level summaries; their full prompt-level scores are not included.

The behavioral report includes an all-pairs zero-shot cohort ($N=36$), a separate held-out zero-shot cohort ($N=32$), and a held-out few-shot cohort ($N=32$). These are distinct evaluations and are not pooled. The dense equal-operand scan covers the seven even target sums $T\in\{4,6,8,10,12,14,16\}$. For each target, it compares the single double prompt $(T/2)+(T/2)$ with the available ordered, unequal, single-digit splits that sum to $T$.

\subsection{Behavioral metrics}

Behavioral competence is measured by full-vocabulary top-1 accuracy and compared with a constant-guess baseline on the same held-out cohort. For prompt $p$ and target token $T$, the neighbor-averaged logit contrast is
\begin{equation}
S(p,T)=\ell_T(p)-\frac{\ell_{T-1}(p)+\ell_{T+1}(p)}{2},
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_j(p)$ is the next-token logit for the leading-space answer token corresponding to $j$. This score measures relative logit preference among the target and its two neighboring values; it is not a measure of exact-answer probability or proof of arithmetic computation.

For each even target $T$, the equal-operand advantage is
\begin{equation}
A_T=S(p_{d,d},T)-\frac{1}{|C_T|}\sum_{(a,b)\in C_T}S(p_{a,b},T),\qquad d=T/2,
\label{eq:adv}
\end{equation}
where $p_{a,b}$ is the prompt \texttt{a + b =} and $C_T$ contains the evaluated ordered unequal splits with $a+b=T$. The reported mean and standard error summarize these seven target-level advantages. The inferential unit is the target level ($n=7$). We use an exact sign-flip test over all $2^7$ sign assignments and a 10,000-resample percentile bootstrap over target levels (seed 20261008). The normalized $A_T/\mathrm{SD}$ quantities remain descriptive and are not significance tests.

The addition-grid analysis records whether the sign of the measured symmetric logit contrast matches target parity. Grid cells share target values and are not independent observations.

\subsection{Attribution and intervention audits}

The saved attribution analyses project residual components onto a target-versus-foil unembedding direction. The audit corrected an earlier calculation by applying final LayerNorm scaling to the residual decomposition before projection and by separately including the unembedding bias term when reconstructing the measured logit contrast. These corrections change the interpretation of selected prompts; attribution alone does not establish necessity or sufficiency.

Intervention summaries compare zero-ablation with mean-ablation and report candidate-component screens with false-discovery-rate-adjusted $q$ values. A separate operator-position patching experiment lacked a corrupt-baseline anchor, so it cannot establish that operator binding was falsified. We keep that experiment distinct from the aggregate operator-string comparisons.

\subsection{Format, operator, and corpus comparisons}

Format and operator analyses are archived as seven matched target-level means per condition, with paired target-level tests and Holm correction. The underlying format/operator prompt-level scores are not included. Operator and connector conditions use the source strings \texttt{+}, Unicode subtraction and multiplication, \texttt{and}, and \texttt{then}. For non-addition strings, the analysis still scores the arithmetic sum token; these comparisons do not test whether the model performs those operations.

The corpus audit uses Infini-gram counts from the Dolma v1.7 index \texttt{v4\_dolma-v1\_7\_llama} as a proxy for possible textual co-occurrence. It queries exact double strings and matched unequal-operand controls. The raw collector applies no eligibility floor and computes no correlation. Its raw request/response records are archived alongside a seven-target no-floor summary. Dolma is not GPT-2's exact training corpus.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The tested cohorts do not show reliable exact-answer performance}

Full-vocabulary top-1 accuracy is 2/36 (5.56\%) on the all-pairs zero-shot cohort and 2/32 (6.25\%) on the held-out zero-shot cohort. On the held-out zero-shot set, the best constant guess scores 8/32 (25\%). Held-out few-shot accuracy is also 2/32. These cohort-specific results do not establish that GPT-2 Small lacks all arithmetic-related computation; they show that the evaluated prompt sets do not support a claim of reliable exact-answer addition above the simple held-out baseline.

\subsection{Parity agreement varies across the addition grid}

In the committed 79-cell grid, target parity predicts the sign of the measured logit contrast in 68/79 cells. Agreement is 63/64 for sums $\leq 13$ and 5/15 for high-sum rows (sums $\geq 14$). Because cells share target sums and have different prompt compositions, these fractions are descriptive rather than independent-trial estimates. The pattern is not uniform across the tested range. In the separate seven-target parity-control comparison, mean advantage is $+0.1865$ (exact sign-flip $p=0.140625$; 95\% bootstrap interval $[-0.0068,+0.3863]$), so this comparison does not reject a zero mean under the stated test.

\subsection{Attribution corrections narrow component interpretations}

For the saved \texttt{3 + 5 =} comparison, corrected residual-component DLA sums to $+0.4658$ and the reported unembedding-bias contrast is $+0.1782$; together they reconstruct $+0.6440$, against a measured logit difference of $+0.6443$. The small discrepancy is retained as reported. In a separate few-shot comparison on \texttt{158 + 274 =}, the dynamic contribution is $+0.8382$ and the bias term is $-1.0303$, against a reported logit difference of $-0.1920$. That contrast compares the leading-space tokens \texttt{8} and \texttt{1}, although the arithmetic answer is 432; it is a token-contrast identity, not evidence that the model computed 8 and was overridden.

Zero-ablation changed the residual-stream scale in the reported prompt. Under mean-ablation across reference prompts, the reported L11H0 shift is $+0.0013$; joint ablation of L11H0 and MLP10 gives $+0.8127$ against an additive prediction of $+0.8131$ on that prompt. These results do not support the earlier suppressor and non-additivity interpretations for the tested prompt and intervention.

The mean advantage is $+0.2474$ across seven target levels, positive at six of seven. An exact target-level sign-flip test gives $p=0.03125$; a 10,000-resample target bootstrap gives a 95\% percentile interval of $[+0.0738,+0.4374]$. The inference is based on seven target-level contrasts and does not establish generalization beyond this grid. The $A_T/\mathrm{SD}$ column remains descriptive, and the result does not identify a mechanism.

Across the seven target levels, the digit+digit versus digit+word paired difference is $-0.2190$ (raw $p=0.015625$, Holm-adjusted $p=0.046875$). The other two format comparisons have Holm-adjusted $p=0.21875$. This does not show a monotonic format effect or identify its source. The available format exports contain target-level means, not every prompt-level formatted score, so pooled dispersions cannot be recomputed from these exports.

Across five operator and connector strings, seven target-level means are available. Plus-versus-other paired tests have Holm-adjusted $p=0.6875$ for all four comparisons. No difference was detected at this sample size; this is not evidence of equivalence. The task is not semantically matched: the same arithmetic sum token is scored under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and connectives, so it does not establish arithmetic under those strings. Prompt-level operator scores are not included.

\subsection{Candidate-component screens do not establish a circuit}

The saved notebook identifies \texttt{10\_mlp\_out}, L9H9, and L10H2 as candidates for further study. The nominated-component causal screen has nominal one-sided $p=0.03125$ for each of the three selected candidates, but one-sided FDR $q=0.09375$ and two-sided FDR $q=0.15$; none passes $q<0.05$. Prospective-range signed advantage change decreases at five targets and increases at two; the clean contrasts at targets 26 and 34 are negative. Transfer is exploratory, and filler prompts also show effects. Target-level completeness ratios are unstable where full effects are small. These screens do not establish necessity, sufficiency, minimality, or a validated addition circuit.

\subsection{Corpus-frequency relationship remains unresolved}

The supplied raw CSV/JSONL export records 79 API attempts, all HTTP 200 with no logged errors. The archived no-floor summary shows positive double-versus-control joint log ratios at all seven targets and mixed conditional log ratios. No association test was run and no eligibility floor was applied, so the corpus counts do not establish whether co-occurrence tracks the model effect. A separate saved output reports 80 attempts and one failure, which remains a distinct unreconciled run. Dolma is a proxy, not GPT-2's training corpus.

\section{Discussion}

The study began with a possible addition-circuit interpretation. The behavioral baseline does not demonstrate reliable exact-answer performance on the tested cohorts. The parity grid shows structured but range-dependent logit preferences. Attribution and intervention audits narrow several component-level explanations. Against this background, the equal-operand scan provides a specific, reproducible prompt-level pattern in the committed score file: a positive mean advantage across seven target sums, with substantial target variation. The cause of this pattern has not been identified.

These findings should be read alongside prior mechanistic studies of arithmetic in pretrained models, including causal mediation, attention/MLP analyses, and heuristic-neuron accounts \cite{stolfo2023causal,yu2024cna,nikankin2025heuristics}. The present measurements do not reproduce or challenge those studies: they address different models and prompt/evaluation setups. The GPT-2 Small greater-than work is the closest model-family comparison, but it validates a circuit for another task \cite{hanna2023greater}. Modular-addition studies provide a complementary controlled-training comparison \cite{nanda2023progress,zhong2023clock}. Accordingly, the result here is a model- and prompt-specific behavioral audit with an exploratory equal-operand contrast, not a new general theory of how language models perform arithmetic.

The most direct follow-up would test whether the equal-operand preference survives independent target selection, prompt-level token and format controls, and preregistered causal interventions. Such a study should retain per-prompt outputs, use matched clean and corrupted baselines, reserve held-out targets, and evaluate both necessity and restoration. A single-operand control and semantically appropriate scoring for operator variants are also needed before interpreting these patterns as arithmetic computations.

\section{Limitations}

The study examines one model, a narrow set of short prompts, and a small number of target sums. Several cohort definitions and few-shot prefixes differ and are not pooled. The supplied clean-rerun exports are archived, including raw corpus API responses, but format and operator exports are target-level summaries rather than full prompt-level score tables. The corpus collector does not perform an association test, and the separate 80/1 saved output remains distinct from the archived 79/0 log. The single-operand comparison remains untested. Candidate-component results are exploratory and cannot support a circuit claim.

The equal-operand advantage is based on one double prompt per target and a small target set; the reported SE is descriptive and does not convert target sums into independent replications. The operator comparisons score the arithmetic-sum token even when the prompt string denotes a different operation. The corpus proxy is not the model's training corpus. These limitations constrain both mechanistic and generalization claims.

\section{Conclusion}

On the evaluated prompts, the evidence does not establish a general addition-specific circuit in GPT-2 Small. Auditing the measurement pipeline weakened several earlier component interpretations. A target-dependent equal-operand preference remains in the saved prompt scores, but the available interventions do not explain it. The appropriate conclusion is neither that GPT-2 Small has been shown to calculate addition through a specific circuit nor that it lacks arithmetic-related computation. The surviving result motivates a more controlled causal study.
